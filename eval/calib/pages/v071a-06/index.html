A handoff, start to finish

StationWhat you doWhat the next person gets
  1. 1

    Open

    Write what the work is and whose station it goes to first.

    They get: A ticket on the rail with their name on it.

    Opened
  2. 2

    Hand on

    Add one line: what you did, and what is left.

    They get: Your note at the top of the ticket, not buried in a thread.

    Waiting
  3. 3

    Sign for it

    The next person stamps the ticket when they pick it up.

    They get: You see it was received, by whom, and when.

    Signed
  4. 4

    File

    The last station closes the ticket.

    They get: The whole route, kept with the ticket for the next time you do this.

    Filed

The rail

Every open ticket hangs in one line, oldest on the left. Monday's standup is reading it left to right.

  • SignedSomeone has picked it up.
  • Waiting onIt is stuck, and the tag says on what.

Small print, on the back of every ticket

Two clicks to hand on

A handoff is a name and a note. There are no status fields to keep current, so the rail stays true without anyone tending it.

Only your team sees the rail

Tickets are visible to the people you invite. A guest you add to one ticket sees that ticket and nothing else.

Same rail at 3 people or 30

When your process gains a step, add a station. Tickets already on the rail pick it up on their next handoff.

Start a board

One email sets it up. You will name your stations and open the first ticket once you are in.

We send a sign-in link, so there is no password to set.
